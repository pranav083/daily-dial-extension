# Make your phone boring

Your phone is engineered to be interesting. Every color, animation, and notification is designed to pull your attention. If you want to check it less, the most reliable approach is not willpower—it's friction. Make the device less rewarding to open, and you'll naturally reach for it less often.

Most phones already have the tools built in. You don't need an app or a special device. You just need to know where the settings live.

## iOS Assistive Access

If you want the strongest possible change, this is it. Assistive Access debuted in iOS 17 and remains largely unknown because it's hidden in the accessibility section.

**What it does:** It rebuilds your entire home screen into a simplified grid showing only the apps you choose. Notifications disappear. Visual clutter vanishes. The phone becomes something more like a specialized tool than a general computer. It is the bluntest of the options here, and the one people are most surprised exists.

**To turn it on:**
1. Open Settings → Accessibility
2. Scroll down and tap Assistive Access
3. Tap Turn On Assistive Access
4. Choose a passcode (not your normal phone passcode)
5. Select which apps appear on your home screen (Messages, Camera, Phone, etc.)
6. Review the simplified layout and confirm

The settings are genuinely restrictive: you cannot see notifications, access Control Center in the normal way, or browse freely between apps. This is the point.

**To exit it:** Triple-click the side button (or home button on older phones) and enter your Assistive Access passcode. You'll return to your normal phone for as long as you need. Triple-click again to go back into simplified mode.

**The catch:** This only works if someone else holds the passcode, or if you accept that one triple-click and your own discipline are the only barriers.

## iOS Screen Time

Screen Time is less powerful than Assistive Access but easier to set up and more flexible.

Go to Settings → Screen Time. You can set:

**App Limits:** Cap time in specific categories (Social Media, Games). When time expires, you'll see a warning, but you can tap "Ignore Limit" to keep using the app. This is a speed bump, not a wall. It makes you choose consciously each time.

**Downtime:** Set a window (say, 9 PM to 8 AM) when only phone calls and your "Always Allowed" apps work. Everything else is greyed out.

**Always Allowed:** Choose which apps bypass Downtime. Most people set this to Phone, Messages, and Emergency.

**The limitation to know:** Limits are easy to bypass. Downtime is more effective, especially if a trusted person holds your Screen Time passcode. Without that, it's a reminder system more than a block.

## Greyscale

Color makes your phone more appealing. Remove it, and the device immediately becomes less exciting to look at. Your brain will register the screen as less rewarding.

**On iOS:**
1. Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size
2. Scroll down to Colour Filters and turn it on
3. Select Greyscale

Then add it to your Accessibility Shortcut so you can toggle it with triple-clicks:
1. Settings → Accessibility → Accessibility Shortcut
2. Check Colour Filters

Now triple-click to switch between color and greyscale instantly.

**On Android:**
Open Digital Wellbeing settings (the exact path varies by manufacturer). You can usually set greyscale to activate during Bedtime mode, or navigate to Developer Options and select "Simulate colour space" → Monochromacy. On some phones, you can use the "Bedtime mode" feature in Digital Wellbeing to turn on greyscale automatically at a set time each evening.

Note: Both approaches work, but the exact menus differ between Android versions and manufacturers. Look for "Bedtime mode," "Digital Wellbeing," or "Display settings" first.

## Android Digital Wellbeing

**Focus mode:** Pause all notifications from apps you choose. You still see them when you manually open the app, but they won't nag you.

**App timers:** Set a daily limit for any app. When time is up, the app icon is greyed out on your home screen until the next day. Unlike iOS, you cannot bypass this without changing your phone's date (which defeats the purpose).

**Bedtime mode:** Enables greyscale and pauses all notifications for a set window. Your screen becomes monochrome and silent after a certain time.

These all live in Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls, though the exact menu names shift between Android versions.

## Home screen surgery

This costs nothing and requires no settings:

- **Remove apps from your home screen** (but don't delete them). Long-press an app, tap "Remove App," then "Remove from Home Screen." It still lives in your app library; you can search for it if you need it. This makes opening it a deliberate act instead of a reflexive tap.

- **Turn off notification badges.** Go to Settings → Notifications, find each app, and disable "Badge App Icon." No red numbers counting your unread messages.

- **Hide notification previews.** In Notifications settings, flip "Show Previews" to "When Unlocked" or "Never." If a notification arrives while your phone is locked, you'll see almost nothing.

- **Use search to open apps.** On iPhone, swipe down to Spotlight and type the app name. On Android, tap the search widget. This adds a few extra seconds and a moment of deliberation.

## The unglamorous option

Put your phone in another room.

This outperforms every setting above because it removes the object itself. You cannot mindlessly reach for your phone if it is not within arm's reach. It also costs nothing and requires no configuration. The only barrier is that you have to actually do it.

Most friction-based approaches work by degrees. This one works by degrees, too—you can still retrieve your phone—but the activation energy is so high that your brain stops trying.
