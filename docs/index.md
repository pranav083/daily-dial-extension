# Where did the day actually go?

Paint your day on a 24-hour dial — pick a category, drag across the hours, and
a full day is logged in about ten seconds. Then see whether the time went where
you meant it to.

Free, open source, and no account. By default it makes no network requests at
all: your data stays in your browser.

[Install from the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/daily-dial/mgcjgngceajnmfhkifccaoeccbmfikhn){: .cta}
[About the extension](about.html){: .cta}

## Taking the day back

Daily Dial's job is to show you where your time went. It deliberately stops
there — it does not block, enforce, or nag, because plenty of tools already do
that part well and do it better than a time logger could.

These guides are the handoff. Each one is about a pattern the dial can show
you, and what people actually do about it. Nothing here is affiliated with
Daily Dial, and nothing is sponsored.

- [**Make your phone boring**](guides/boring-phone.html) — the accessibility
  and Screen Time features already on your iPhone or Android that make the
  device less compelling, set up step by step.
- [**Block sites during the hours you want to work**](guides/blocking-sites.html)
  — SelfControl, Cold Turkey, Freedom, and why an all-day block gets switched
  off by lunchtime.
- [**Protect your best hour**](guides/best-hour.html) — finding your most
  productive hour in your own data, then defending it.
- [**Rest that actually happens**](guides/rest.html) — break timers, and the
  difference between rest you intended and rest you took.

## The honest limits

- Reminders only fire while Chrome is running.
- Data is per browser profile; there is no sync unless you turn on the
  optional Google Drive backup.
- The dial has 15-minute resolution.
